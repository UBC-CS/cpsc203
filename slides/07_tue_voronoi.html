Voronoi Diagrams

Voronoi diagrams

Given a (finite) set of “centres” \(c_{1}, c_{2}, \ldots, c_{k}\), a Voronoi region, \(R_{j}\) consists of the set of points nearer to centre \(c_{j}\), than to any other centre.

Together, the \(R_{j}\) regions compose the Voronoi diagram of a plane.

Every point in plane is in some region and no point is in 2 regions.

Applications

  • Biology, Ecology, Forestry: Model and analyze plant competition.
  • Zoology: Model and analyze the territories of animals.
  • Robotics: Path planning in the presence of obstacles.
  • Astronomy: Identify clusters of stars and clusters of galaxies.
  • Cartography: Piece together satellite photographs into large “mosaic” maps.
  • Geography: Analyzing patterns of urban settlements.
  • Marketing: Model market of US metropolitan areas.
  • Metallurgy: Modelling “grain growth” in metal films.

More Applications

  • Physiology: Analysis of capillary distribution in cross-sections of muscle tissue to compute oxygen transport (“Capillary domains”).
  • Meteorology: Estimate regional rainfall averages, given data at discrete rain gauges.
  • Anthropology and Archeology: Identify regions under the influence of different neolithic clans, chiefdoms, ceremonial centers, or hill forts.
  • Crystallography and Chemistry: Modelling alloy structures as sphere packings (“Domain of an atom”).
  • Geology: Estimation of ore reserves in a deposit using info obtained from bore holes.

Algorithmic Art

  1. Select a subset of points from the original image.
  2. Use those points, with their colors, as centers in a new image of the same size.
  3. Build the Voronoi diagram in the new image, using the center colors from the original image.

We are going to focus on calculating Voronoi regions over discrete domains, specifically images.

Choose some pixels as centers. For every other pixel, figure out which center is closest. For that operation, we need to compute distance between pixels (or some monotonically related measure).

Distance Between Pixels

Draw in the right angle triangle.

Give the pixels coordinates and write out the formula \(\sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2}\).

Note that the location of the origin doesn’t matter.

Nearest Center

Note that we don’t need the exact distance, just which center is closer to yellow.

In particular, we don’t need to compute the square root, which is computationally expensive (compared to other arithmetic operations) and results in floating point numbers (ugly).

  1. \(O(n)\).

  3. \(O(dn^2)\).
  4. \(O(n)\).

Can we do better? Do we need to “visit” (do something for) every pixel? Yes: At a minimum we need to write out a colour for that pixel. So, we probably can’t avoid \(O(n)\). But what about the dependence on number of centers? Can we avoid doing something for each pixel-center combination?
