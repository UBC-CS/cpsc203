Your turn: Thinking about bits

  • What’s the largest number you can represent with 4 bits?
  • How many bits do you need to represent 120?
  • If I give you one more bit, what happens to how many numbers you can represent?
  • How high can you count on your fingers? and toes?
  • As the number \(k\) grows, how does the number of digits grow?
  • Largest with 4 bits: 15 (1111).
  • Bits for 120: 7 bits (120 < 128 = 2^7).
  • One more bit: DOUBLES the numbers you can represent!
  • Remember 210 about 1000, 220 = 106.
  • Complexity (number of digits) grows as \(\log k\).
  • What about non-positional number systems?
    • Tally or roman numerals grow as \(k\) (even for Roman numerals, since the largest single digit is “M” = one thousand).

Colour

Colour Interpretation

How does light wavelength become colour? Biological interpretation

What’s your favorite colour? Psychological interpretation

Does that colour influence your dress/decor/purchases? Cultural interpretation

Normal computer images do none of these three things (although it does take advantage of how human biology detects light).

Human Colour Perception

  • Human color vision has three channels
    • Our brain experiences color based on the intensity of light detected by “red”, “green” and “blue” cone cells in our retinas
  • If we are shown a yellow wavelength
    • L cones will respond strongly
    • M cones will respond almost as strongly
    • S cones will respond weakly
  • To fake a yellow wavelength (for a human)
    • Show a lot of a red wavelength
    • Show a little less of a green wavelength
    • Show little or no blue wavelength


Colour Representation

  • So we only need to figure out how to manage blends of three colors!

  • Can we use integers to represent colours?

    • One way: RGB or (red, green, blue), where each “component” is integer in the range 0 through 255.
    • For example: (255, 0, 127) == ?
    • Fun calculator for colour values: https://colorizer.org

  • Why 256 possible values?

  • Early computer colour might only use 5 bits / channel (so slightly less than 16 bits).
    • 8 bits / channel is convenient as (1) computers actually work with data on a byte (or even word = 32 or 64 bit) level and (2) it is sufficient that humans don’t notice the gradations.

Aside: Other Colour Representations

  • There are alternative ways to represent colour.
    • HSV (hue, saturation, value) is closer to how humans usually pick colours.
    • CMY (cyan, magenta, yellow) for reflective colour.
    • Sometimes you want more than three channels:
      • “Alpha” for controlling transparency.
      • CMYK to allow for proper black.
      • Additional wavelengths for sensors operating outside human capability.
  • HSV:
    • Hue (H): The color family, measured in degrees from 0° to 360° on a color wheel (0° is red, 120° is green, and 240° is blue).
    • Saturation (S): The purity or intensity of the color, ranging from 0% (gray) to 100% (vivid, pure color).
    • Value (V): The brightness or lightness of the color, ranging from 0% (absolute black) to 100% (full brightness).
  • CMYK is used for printers.
  • “Multi-spectral” images are used widely in geosensing and astronomy.
